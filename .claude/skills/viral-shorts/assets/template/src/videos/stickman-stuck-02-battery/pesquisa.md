## Central question
Where does a power bank keep its energy, and how does it get out?

## How it works, step by step
1. **The parts.** A lithium-ion cell has an anode (usually graphite) and a cathode (a lithium metal oxide), an electrolyte between them, a porous separator in the middle and two current collectors [1][2][3][5].
2. **Ions inside.** The electrolyte carries positively charged lithium ions from one electrode to the other, through the separator [1][3][4].
3. **Electrons outside.** The separator keeps the electrodes apart and does not let electrons through, so the electrons released at the anode have to travel through the outside wire, through the device being powered, to reach the cathode [3][4].
4. **Discharge.** While the battery powers something, lithium ions move from the anode to the cathode inside, and electrons flow through the device outside [1][3][4].
5. **Charging.** A charger pushes the process backwards: lithium ions return to the graphite anode and electrons flow back through the outside circuit [3][4][5].
6. **Why it can be recharged many times.** The lithium ions slip in and out of the layers of the electrodes without destroying them [5].

## Surprising facts (hook candidates)
- Inside the battery, the electrons never cross the middle: the separator forces them to take "the long way" through your device [3][4].
- The 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded for the development of lithium-ion batteries [5]. *(Not used in the script.)*

## Myths and mistakes
- "A battery is full of electricity." It stores chemical energy; the current only exists while ions move inside and electrons move outside [1][3].
- Battery life, number of cycles and charging habits vary between products and sources, so **the script leaves them out**.
