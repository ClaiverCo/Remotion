## Central question
How does ordinary sand end up as the "brain" of a phone?

## How it works, step by step
1. **Silica sand.** Chipmakers start from silica sand (silicon dioxide), usually quarried [1][3].
2. **Purify into silicon.** The sand is melted and refined until almost pure silicon is extracted; electronic-grade silicon is extremely pure [1][3].
3. **Grow a crystal.** A small seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon and slowly pulled up while it rotates, growing one large cylindrical crystal, an ingot (the Czochralski method) [1][4].
4. **Slice into wafers.** The ingot is cut into thin, uniform discs called wafers with diamond saws [1][4].
5. **Print with light.** The wafer is coated with a light-sensitive film; light is shone through a mask, like a stencil, and the optics shrink the pattern onto the wafer. Etching and new thin layers follow [2][5].
6. **Layer after layer.** That cycle is repeated many times (dozens of layers) to build the circuits [2][5].
7. **Transistors.** The circuits are made of transistors, tiny switches that turn a current on or off [1][6]. A modern smartphone chip holds billions of them: for example, 15 billion in the A15 and 19 billion in the A17 Pro [7].

## Surprising facts (hook candidates)
- The most advanced machines print with extreme ultraviolet light, at a wavelength of 13.5 nanometres [5]. *(Not used in the script.)*
- Turning sand into a chip takes more than 300 steps [3]. *(Not used in the script.)*

## Myths and mistakes
- "Any beach sand becomes chips." Chipmakers use silica sand that is purified to an extreme degree; the script just says "sand" and never says "beach" [1][3].
- Exact purity percentages, ingot sizes and step counts vary between sources, so **the script leaves them out**.
